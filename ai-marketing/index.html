Persona

Marcus Rivera

Owner · Rivera Landscape Group · Chicago suburbs · 20 vehicles · 3 locations

Who He Is

Marcus's father founded Rivera Landscape Group thirty years ago. Marcus grew up in it as a teenager — riding routes, learning the business — then drifted away in his twenties. He returned at 35 when his father's health began to fail. He didn't come back to be a caretaker. He came back to prove something.

What He Believes
  • His routes cross more than they should. He sees it — a truck headed east passing one headed west on the same street.
  • He's leaving money on the table that he can't see clearly enough to pick up.
  • The business ran fine under his dad. Running fine isn't good enough anymore.
His Core Worry

Marcus worries he's managing the business instead of growing it. Every week without a clear picture of route efficiency feels like margin he can't get back. He's not worried about the cost of a tool. He's worried about the cost of staying stuck.

What Growth Means to Him

Growth means taking what his father built — a real, durable business — and making it undeniably bigger and better. It means being the one who modernized it. It means his dad seeing it happen.

Survey

Three questions. Two minutes.

Your answers shape how we follow up. The more you share, the more useful the conversation that follows.

Question 1 — Route Visibility

Most fleet owners we talk to describe the same thing: a gut feeling that routes could be tighter, but no easy way to see it. When a driver calls out sick or a stop gets added last minute, it usually means someone's winging it. Sound familiar?

Question 2 — Growth vs. Headcount

When business grows, the instinct is to add a truck or hire a driver. But sometimes the capacity is already there — buried in inefficient routing. Have you ever wondered if you could handle more volume without adding overhead?

Question 3 — Daily Operational Visibility

On any given day, do you know roughly where your trucks are and how routes are progressing — or does clarity only come after the fact?

Want two more?

You're finding this useful — we have a couple of additional questions if you'd like to keep going.

Discussion

Open questions for reviewers of this proof-of-concept. No right answers — the goal is to surface your reactions and instincts.

  1. Does the growth frame land differently than an efficiency pitch for this buyer? Most B2B software leads with ROI and cost savings. This page leads with legacy and ambition. Which resonates more — and why?
  2. Is the pickleball racket disconnect a strength or a liability in B2B? The gift has nothing to do with the product. That's intentional. Does it work in a business context, or does it feel out of place?
  3. At what point in the survey does it feel like a sales funnel vs. genuine research? Question 1 describes a shared problem. Question 2 hints that capacity might already exist. Where does Marcus shift from "they get me" to "they're selling me"?
  4. Would Marcus fill this out? What would make him stop? Be specific. What's the sentence, the question, the word that loses him?
  5. What's missing from the Marcus persona that would change the pitch? Industry, geography, and fleet size are here. What else matters — and what would you add?
  6. How does B2B AI personalization feel different from the B2C version? Compare this to the Jake & Mia version at twodogplayground.com. What changes when the buyer is a business owner vs. a consumer?
Facilitation Notes
  • No slides, no pitch — this is a conversation
  • All feedback is useful, including skepticism
  • Bring your own business context
  • Questions don't need answers — they need better questions

⛔ Implementation Detail: Prospect Identification

In production, prospect identification works as follows:

  • Fleet owner data sourced from industry associations, fleet registration records, LinkedIn, and business credit databases
  • Each prospect scored on fleet size, geographic density, years in operation, and presence/absence of known route software
  • Marcus appears on this list because he fits the target profile: 15+ vehicles, Chicago metro, landscaping, no known routing tool in use

This scoring and list-building infrastructure does not exist in this proof-of-concept.

⛔ Implementation Detail: Prospect List Building

In production, the mailing list is compiled from:

  • Landscaping industry associations and trade directories
  • Fleet registration data (15+ vehicles, Chicago metro)
  • LinkedIn enrichment for owner identity and tenure
  • Business credit signals for company health and growth indicators

Each package is addressed to the owner by name and shipped to the business address on record via a fulfillment partner — no manual handling.

This list and fulfillment infrastructure does not exist in this proof-of-concept.

⛔ Implementation Detail: Time Lag Tracking

In production, the system tracks three timestamps:

  • Mail drop date — when the package was sent to Marcus's address
  • Page hit timestamp — when Marcus first visited this URL
  • Survey completion or abandonment — which questions were answered and when

The gap between drop date and page visit is a strong buying-intent signal. Same-day = interrupted something and acted immediately. Three weeks later = it sat on his desk and he came back — also a strong signal, different in kind.

This tracking infrastructure does not exist in this proof-of-concept.

⛔ Implementation Detail: AI-Generated Messages

In production, every message in this sequence is generated by AI based on:

  • Marcus's survey responses — checkbox selections and open-text answers
  • Page behavior — time on page, sections visited, where he abandoned the survey
  • Prospect profile — fleet size, industry, location, years in business
  • Time lag signals — when he hit the page relative to the mail drop

No message is a template. Each is written fresh at time of sending. The AI is instructed to stay in the growth frame — never pitch cost savings, always pitch visibility and scale.

The AI generation infrastructure does not exist in this proof-of-concept. Messages shown are illustrative.

⛔ Implementation Detail: Survey Data Capture

In production, survey submission triggers:

  • Responses stored in CRM, linked to Marcus's prospect record
  • AI scores responses for buying intent and routes to the appropriate follow-up sequence
  • Open-text answers are the highest-value input — used to generate hyper-personalized outreach copy
  • Follow-up sequence initiates within 24 hours of submission
  • Partial completions (abandonment after Q1 or Q2) trigger a separate, softer nudge sequence

This CRM and automation infrastructure does not exist in this proof-of-concept.
